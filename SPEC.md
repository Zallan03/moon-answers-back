## Final text (108 characters)

## Why this one wins (judge criteria)

1. **Speaks as the Moon answering** — first-person Moon, directly addressing everyone who stares up, wishes and howls; it answers all three things the brief names (v2 added "and howls").
2. **Funny on one read** — the grievance is petty and human: four billion years of service, and nobody ever wished the Moon a happy birthday.
3. **Moon's own voice** — wry, old, a little tired of being wished upon; "I see how it is" is the weary comeback of something ancient that has finally had enough.
4. **Comeback lands before the line ends** — the turn ("not one of you ever wished me...") arrives mid-line and the final sentence sharpens it.
5. **Tight** — 108 of 240 characters, none wasted.

## Candidates rejected

- "You howl at me, wish on me, and blame me for your tides. I am a rock. I did not sign up for this." — covers the brief well and "I am a rock" is a nice flat turn, but the birthday grievance is a sharper, more specific joke; "did not sign up for this" is a familiar phrasing.
- "You keep promising me you'll change 'starting Monday.' I've watched a thousand Mondays." — ties to Monday nicely, but it needs the reader to know the Monday trope and the punchline is weaker than the birthday one.
- "I have listened to four billion years of wishes. Yours was the first one about a parking space. Congratulations." — funny and specific, but it addresses one wisher instead of all of humanity, so it answers the brief less fully.
